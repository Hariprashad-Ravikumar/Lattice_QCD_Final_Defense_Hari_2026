\section{Conclusions}

% Slide 49
\begin{frame}{Conclusions}
  \begin{columns}[t]
    \begin{column}{0.53\textwidth}
      \begin{block}{Results}
        \begin{itemize}
          \item This thesis reconstructs the $x$-dependence of the Sivers shift from lattice data using a fitted model: scan $\bvec\tcdot P\neq0$ with off-axis staples, model with a Fourier-transformable power law, extrapolate $\zetahat\to\infty$.
          \item $\vec v_T\perp\vec b_T$ satisfies the kinematic constraint; symmetry improvement and interpolation give the off-axis staples.
          \item The tested Gaussian ansatz fails; a coupled power law describes all three amplitudes.
          \item The extrapolated model has relative factorization residuals of 5.7--7.6\% over the tested $x$ and transverse-separation domains.
        \end{itemize}
      \end{block}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.45\textwidth}
      \begin{alertblock}{Limitations}
        \begin{itemize}
          \item Bands statistical (Jackknife); model dependence explored, not propagated.
          \item Small $x$ depends on the fitted large-$\bvec\tcdot P$ tail.
          \item $\eta\to\infty$ through the asymptotic $c(\eta)$ model; $\zetahat\to\infty$ by extrapolation ($\zetahat\le0.79$).
          \item One ensemble: no continuum, volume or quark-mass study. Global fits include Gaussian priors.
          \item $|\vprp b|\ge 3a$; $\tAmp_{12B}^{\text{Im}}$ set to zero.
        \end{itemize}
      \end{alertblock}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}
